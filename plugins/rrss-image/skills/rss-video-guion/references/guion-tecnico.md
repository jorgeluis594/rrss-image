# Technical script

The technical script turns the story into scenes that production can film or
generate and editing can assemble without guessing. Write every field from the
scene template in SKILL.md; use the vocabulary below so all three skills read
the same words the same way.

## Scene functions

`gancho`, `contexto`, `giro`, `prueba`, `resultado`, `cta`. A scene may combine
`resultado` and `cta` in short videos. Every video has exactly one `gancho` scene
first and ends with `cta` or a `resultado` that contains the CTA.

## Shot vocabulary

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| `plano` | `general` (place, context), `medio` (waist up), `medio-corto` (chest up), `primer-plano` (face), `detalle` (hands, product, texture), `cenital` (from above), `pov` (what the character sees) |
| `angulo` | `frontal-ojos`, `tres-cuartos` (30–45°), `lateral`, `picado` (from above), `contrapicado` (from below) |
| `movimiento` | `fijo`, `punch-in` (digital zoom on a cut), `push-lento` (slow zoom in), `paneo`, `mano` (handheld), `seguimiento`, `timelapse`, `camara-lenta` |
| `transicion` | `corte`, `jump-cut`, `match-cut` (cut on a matching movement or shape), `l-cut` / `j-cut` (audio overlaps the cut), `barrido` (whip), `tapado` (hand or object covers the lens) |

## Pacing

- Something visible changes every 2–4 seconds: shot size, angle, B-roll, text, or a
  sound accent. Sources disagree on the exact interval; 2–4 s is the working default.
- The same shot never stays more than 4 s. A person talking to camera gets B-roll
  or an angle change at least every 3–4 s, without breaking their sentence.
- Every claim in a line has a scene element that shows it (action, detail, result).
- Slow motion only at the payoff moment. Long processes are compressed with
  timelapse, speed ramps, or match cuts.
- Avoid planning effects for their own sake: endless zooms and whooshes tire viewers.

## Timing budget

- Spoken Spanish at a natural, energetic pace is about 2.5 words per second
  (approximate). A 3-second hook holds about 7 spoken words.
- Each scene's `linea` must fit its duration at that rate. If not, shorten the line,
  split the scene, or extend it while keeping the rhythm rules.
- The sum of scene durations lands within ±10% of `duracion_objetivo`.
- Defaults by intent when the user delegates length: reach 15–30 s, promotion or ad
  15–30 s, educational or story 30–60 s. Go past 60 s only when the user asks.

## On-screen text

- `texto_pantalla` is short: the hook in 3–8 words, then keywords, numbers, or the CTA.
  Subtitles of the spoken line are produced in editing and are not written here.
- TikTok recommends no more than 5–10 words per second of on-screen reading.
- Text lives inside the central area of a 1080×1920 frame: keep about 250 px clear at
  the bottom, 100–250 px at the top, and 150 px on the right for platform controls.
  These are approximate and change with the apps; editing verifies them.
- Text with prices, offers, or conditions is copied exactly from the brief.

## Audio

`audio` describes intent, not files: music mood and energy, where a beat or drop
lands, sound effects at scene accents (pop for text, whoosh for transitions, the real
sound of the product), and ambient sound worth capturing. Brand accounts use music
with a commercial license; name a trend sound only when the user confirms it can be
used commercially. Voice is always intelligible over music.

## Source of each scene

- `grabación`: filmed with real people, places, and product.
- `generado`: produced with an image or video model. Requires real product references
  when the product appears; generated people are never presented as real customers or
  staff. Prefer it for scenes that are expensive, impossible, or purely illustrative.
- `material-existente`: footage or photos the brand already owns; name where it is.

When the user has not decided, propose the source per scene with a reason and ask.

## Coverage

`cobertura` lists the extra shots production must capture beyond the main shot, so
editing has options. Minimum for a scene with a spoken line: the main shot plus one
other angle or size (for example `tres-cuartos`, `detalle`). Recommended across the
video: frontal medium, three-quarter, product detail, POV, wide establishing shot,
overhead when there is a table or product, and a reaction.

## Talent direction

`intencion` says what the person feels and wants in the scene in plain words
(«apurada pero divertida, quiere que la ayuden»), not acting jargon. Natural lines
(`literal: no`) are written as a suggestion in the brand's register; the actor can
adapt them. Literal lines are short and easy to say in one breath.
