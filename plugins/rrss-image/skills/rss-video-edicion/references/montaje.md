# Cutting for retention

Editing a short video means the viewer can never predict the next second: something
visible changes every 2–4 seconds without breaking the sentence being spoken. The
techniques below serve the story in `VIDEO.md`; none is applied for its own sake.

## Opening and ending

- Frame 0 is the hook's first frame: movement or a face, with the hook text already
  on screen. No fade from black, logo, or intro animation.
- The hook's premise is clear by second 3. Trim any warm-up from the selected take.
- The ending holds the CTA long enough to read (at least 1.5 s) and, when the script
  plans it, cuts back to the opening image or line so the video loops.

## Techniques

| Technique | How to apply |
| --- | --- |
| Jump cut | Remove silences, false starts, and filler («eh», «este») inside a take. Leave 2–4 frames of air so speech does not sound chopped. |
| Angle change | Alternate the main shot and coverage (`E<NN>-A`, `E<NN>-B`) at a new sentence or idea. |
| Punch-in | On a cut, scale the same shot to 115–135% keeping the eyes at the same height, as a fake second angle. From 4K sources this keeps full sharpness at 1080×1920. |
| Slow push | 103–112% over 2–6 s with ease in-out, for tension or a reveal. |
| B-roll over voice | Cover a talking head with the product, hands, place, or result while the voice continues. No talking head stays more than 3–4 s uncovered. |
| L-cut / J-cut | Let the audio of one shot start before or continue after the picture cut, so cuts feel natural. |
| Match cut | Cut on a matching movement or shape: a turn, a hand covering the lens, a snap for before/after. |
| Cut on action | Cut in the middle of a movement, not before or after it, so the cut hides itself. |
| Speed | Speed ramps or timelapse for long processes; slow motion only at the payoff. |
| Pattern interrupt | A text pop, a sound accent, a still frame, or a size change when a section risks feeling flat. |

## Rhythm checks

- No shot lasts more than 4 s; the visual change interval averages 2–4 s.
- Every claim spoken has its visual proof on screen at that moment.
- Cuts without voice land on the music's beat.
- Continuity: eyeline, wardrobe, product state, and screen direction match across
  cuts; mismatches are cut around with coverage.
- Over-editing check: if zooms, whooshes, or effects appear on most cuts, remove the
  ones that do not serve a specific moment. Constant effects tire viewers.

## Working with the real material

- The real takes decide the timing; the script's times are the target. Natural lines
  (`literal: no`) were said in the actor's words: cut what was said, not the script.
- Use the in/out points marked in `material.md` for generated clips.
- If the total runs long, cut pauses and redundant words first, then shorten B-roll,
  and only then propose removing a beat, which is a story change for the user.
