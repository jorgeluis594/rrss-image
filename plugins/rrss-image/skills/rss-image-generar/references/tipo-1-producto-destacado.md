# 1. Producto destacado

- Input: a real photo and an introductory message or product quality supported by the brief.
- Structure: one recognizable main product, visually separated from the background,
  with room for a headline and brand. Text and secondary objects do not cover identifying features.
- Background: apply [Background treatment](fondos.md) before defining it. If the user
  describes a situation or action, ask about the background, its intent, and missing
  characteristics, then wait for the answer before producing. An action does not define
  the setting. Respect supplied details and complete only delegated decisions, keeping
  the product as the first focal point. Do not automatically reclassify it as Producto en contexto.
- Hierarchy: product → headline → brand and action, if the action is part of the brief.
- Framing: show shape and packaging for an introduction; show the actual detail when
  the message concerns texture or finish. Do not invent features absent from references.
- Validation: the product is the first focal point and recognizable at viewing size.
  If a price appears, it has secondary prominence.

Producto destacado has two variants; ask which one before producing and record it.
The rules above apply to both.

## 1a. Destacado clásico

Product, headline, and brand as described above.

## 1b. Anatomía del producto

- Input: a real photo where every labeled part is clearly visible; headline and
  optional subtitle; optional background word (usually the product or flavor name).
- Labels supplied by the user: use them without asking for confirmation. Check each
  against the reference; ask only about a label that is not visible or is a claim.
- Label proposal (when the user supplied none): analyze the reference image and
  propose in Spanish two to four visible parts to highlight, each with its label and
  where the line will point (e.g. «CREMA → copete de crema batida»). Wait for the
  user's confirmation or changes; do not generate until the list is confirmed.
- Record the final label list and its source (user or confirmed proposal) in `direction.md`.
- Labels: one or two words that name a part or attribute visible in the reference.
  A label that cannot be checked by looking at
  the photo is a claim or benefit ("sin azúcar", "más resistente") and belongs to
  Producto con argumentos; do not include it here. Do not invent parts, layers, or
  toppings to have something to label.
- Structure: large centered product (about 50–60% of the height) on a surface with
  studio light. Thin single-color leader lines end in a dot exactly on the named
  detail; labels sit outside the product, balanced left and right. Lines and labels
  do not cross identifying features (logo, packaging label, main silhouette).
- Background word: behind the product and partly covered by it, in a tone close to
  the background so it reads as texture; it may repeat in one or two rows and must
  not compete with the headline.
- Palette: background, surface, and typography derived from the product's own tones,
  plus one light tone for the headline. Keep product colors intact.
- Hierarchy: headline → product → labels → background word → logo.
- Validation: each line ends on the correct part; each label matches something
  visible in the reference and is spelled correctly and readable at feed size;
  labels do not overlap or crowd; the background word does not cover the product.
- Not suitable when the product has no distinct visible parts (plain bottle, closed
  box) or when the message is benefits; suggest 1a or type 4 respectively.
